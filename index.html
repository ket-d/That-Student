BE THAT STUDENT WHO STAND OUTS

Introduction

We live in a world full of opportunities.

Today, students can apply for scholarships, research internships, fellowships, exchange programs, leadership initiatives, competitions, and jobs across the globe. Many of these opportunities are open to students regardless of where they come from or where they study.

Yet, while opportunities have become more accessible, competition has also become greater.

Every year, thousands of students apply for the same opportunities. Some are selected, while many are not. In most cases, the difference is not simply intelligence, t is preparation. The students who stand out are often those who have intentionally developed themselves beyond the minimum requirements.

That raises an important question:

What does it really mean to stand out?

Standing out is not about comparing yourself with students at Harvard, Oxford, or any other world-class institution. It is not about proving that you are better than everyone else. It is about becoming the best version of yourself and distinguishing yourself among those who share similar opportunities and circumstances. Excellence begins where you are.

This book was written to help you understand what it means to stand out and, more importantly, how to do it. It will challenge you to pursue academic excellence, develop valuable skills, gain meaningful experience, take initiative, and build a reputation for excellence your field.

If you dream of making the most of your education and unlocking the opportunities available to you, this book will provide the principles and practical steps to help you stand out.

The goal is simple: not just to become a successful student, but to become a student who stands out.

The principles in this book will help you distinguish yourself, but every journey begins with understanding the destination. Before learning how to stand out, you must first understand what it truly means to stand out.

Chapter 1: What Does It Mean to Stand Out?

The phrase "stand out" is used so often that many people never stop to think about what it really means. Some believe it means being the smartest student in the class. Others think it is about being popular, winning every competition, or constantly receiving recognition.

But standing out is much deeper than that.

Standing out is not about drawing attention to yourself. It is about becoming the kind of person whose character, knowledge, skills, and actions naturally distinguish them from others. It is the result of intentional growth, consistent excellence, and a commitment to becoming the best version of yourself.

Standing out is not about competing with students from Harvard, Oxford, or any other world-class institution. It is about distinguishing yourself among students who share similar opportunities and circumstances

I learned this lesson during my research internship in Switzerland.

One day, my supervisor asked me, "Ketura, how do you feel being here in this research lab?"

Without hesitation, I replied, "Professor, to be sincere with you, I feel inadequate."

He smiled and asked me another question.

"How do you feel when you are at your university in Cameroon?"

I answered, "I feel sufficient."

He then gave me advice that has stayed with me ever since.

He said, "Don't compare yourself to the standards of the world. Start from your school."

At that moment, I realized I had been measuring myself against people who had different backgrounds, opportunities, and experiences. Instead of appreciating how far I had come, I was focusing on how far I still had to go.

That conversation changed the way I viewed excellence. I understood that standing out is not about comparing yourself with students from the world's best universities. It is about making the most of the opportunities available to you and distinguishing yourself among those who share similar circumstances. Once you have done that, you can continue raising your standards and competing on bigger stages.

Excellence begins where you are.

Standing out begins where you are.

The real question is not, "Am I as good as students at the world's best universities?" The better question is, "How am I growing compared with the opportunities and resources available to me?"

A student who consistently learns, develops new skills, serves others, and takes initiative will naturally stand out among peers who simply do the minimum. Excellence is measured by growth, commitment, and consistency—not by prestige.

Standing out also means becoming known for something valuable. When people hear your name, what comes to mind? Do they think of integrity, hard work, leadership, reliability, creativity, curiosity, or a willingness to help others? These qualities often leave a stronger and more lasting impression than grades alone.

History is filled with people who distinguished themselves not because they had the greatest advantages, but because they made the most of the opportunities they had. They were prepared when opportunities came, remained committed during challenges, and consistently pursued excellence in their work.

Standing out is not a destination; it is a lifestyle. It is the daily decision to grow, to learn, to serve, and to improve yourself. It is about becoming someone who creates value wherever they are.

Before moving to the next chapter, remember this definition:

Standing out is the intentional pursuit of excellence in your character, knowledge, skills, and service so that you become a person of value and positive impact.

Reflection

Take a moment to think about your own journey as a student.

When you hear the words stand out, what comes to your mind? Have you been measuring yourself against students in other universities, or have you been focusing on becoming the best version of yourself where you are?

Ask yourself:

  • What makes me different from my classmates?
  • Beyond my grades, what value am I developing?
  • If someone described me in three words, what would they say?

Remember, standing out is not about trying to impress others. It is about intentionally becoming the student you are capable of being.

My Takeaway

I have learned that standing out is not about being perfect or competing with everyone around me. It is about making the most of the opportunities available to me and choosing to grow every day.

From today, I choose to be intentional about my growth, my learning, and my character. I will focus on becoming the best version of myself, one step at a time.

Chapter 2: Why Should You Stand Out?

Standing out begins with a strong academic foundation. As students, our first responsibility is to learn, grow intellectually, and pursue academic excellence. Good grades reflect discipline, commitment, and a willingness to do your best. They open doors to scholarships, graduate programs, and many other opportunities.

However, in today's competitive world, academic excellence alone is often not enough.

Every year, thousands of students graduate with excellent grades. Many apply for the same scholarships, internships, research positions, leadership programs, and jobs. While academic performance remains important, selection committees often look beyond transcripts. They ask, What else makes this student stand out?

Have they developed practical skills? Have they gained relevant experience? Have they taken initiative? Have they demonstrated leadership, curiosity, or a commitment to continuous learning?

This does not mean grades no longer matter. On the contrary, academic excellence remains one of the strongest foundations a student can build. But it should be complemented by skills, experience, and a willingness to grow beyond the classroom.

Think of academic excellence as the foundation of a house. A strong foundation is essential, but a house is not complete with only a foundation. It also needs walls, a roof, doors, and windows. In the same way, your education becomes stronger when it is supported by practical experience, valuable skills, initiative, and good character.

Standing out is therefore about becoming a well-rounded student. It is about combining academic excellence with continuous personal and professional growth.

When opportunities arise, those who have prepared themselves beyond the minimum requirements are often the ones who are remembered.

The purpose of standing out is not to outshine others. It is to become the best version of yourself, maximize the opportunities available to you, and make a meaningful contribution wherever you go.

Reflection

  • Am I doing my best academically?
  • Beyond my grades, what am I doing to prepare for future opportunities?
  • If someone looked at my academic record and my experiences together, would they see a well-rounded student?

Is there something I am doing that distinguishes me from my peers?

Be honest with yourself. Your answers are not meant to discourage you but to help you identify areas where you can grow. Remember, standing out is not achieved in a single day—it is built through consistent choices and intentional effort.

My Takeaway

Academic excellence is the foundation of my success, but it is not the finish line. I will continue to pursue excellent grades while intentionally developing the skills, experiences, and character that will prepare me for the opportunities ahead. I choose to become a student who is not only knowledgeable but also well-rounded, prepared, and ready for the opportunities of tomorrow.

Chapter 3: When Should You Stand Out?

One of the saddest moments for many students comes after graduation.

The excitement of earning a degree slowly fades, and a new question begins to take over:

"What next?"

Many graduates become frustrated because they realize they spent years in school attending lectures, writing examinations, and passing courses, but very little time preparing for life after university. They discover that employers are looking for skills they never developed, internships they never pursued, leadership experiences they never gained, and practical experience they never thought they needed.

The truth is, the frustration does not begin after graduation—it begins during university. It begins when students choose to blend into the crowd instead of distinguishing themselves. It begins when they are not intentional about their growth and postpone their personal and professional development, believing they have plenty of time.

That is why the best time to stand out is now.

Do not wait until your final year to build your CV. Do not wait until graduation to learn a new skill. Do not wait until an opportunity appears before you begin preparing for it. The opportunities of tomorrow are often won by the preparation of today.

The habits you build today will determine the opportunities you access tomorrow. Every lecture you take seriously, every skill you learn, every meaningful friendship you build, every internship you pursue, and every initiative you take is an investment in your future.

When graduation finally comes, you will not be asking, "What next?" Instead, you will be asking, "Which opportunity should I choose?"

The best time to stand out is while you are still a student. Don't graduate with only a degree. Graduate with direction, skills, experience, and a reputation for excellence.

Reflection

Take a moment to think about your life as a student.

  • Am I intentionally preparing for life after university, or am I only focused on passing my courses?
  • Am I making choices today that my future self will thank me for?
  • What skills, experiences, or habits do I need to start building today?

Remember, the future is not built after graduation, it is built during your years as a student.

My Takeaway

I understand that the best time to prepare for my future is now. I will not wait until graduation to become the student I aspire to be. Every lecture I attend, every skill I learn, every meaningful relationship I build, and every opportunity I pursue is preparing me for the future I desire. I choose to stand out today so that I can step into tomorrow with confidence rather than uncertainty.

Chapter 4: How to Stand Out

By now, you understand what it means to stand out, why it matters, and why the best time to begin is while you are still a student.

So, How do you actually stand out? Standing out is about intentionally developing yourself beyond the minimum requirements in ways that prepare you for the opportunities you desire.

Ask yourself this question:

What am I doing that most of my classmates are not doing?

Perhaps they only study for examinations, while you study to truly understand. Perhaps they go home after lectures, while you attend seminars, learn a new skill, volunteer in a research lab, or work on a personal project. Perhaps they wait for opportunities to find them, while you actively search and prepare for them.

Standing out often begins with doing the things that others overlook but that contribute to your growth. Over the years, I have learned that students who consistently distinguish themselves have one thing in common: they are intentional. They do not leave their growth to chance. They make deliberate decisions that shape the kind of student—and ultimately the kind of professional—they become.

The following principles have guided my own journey and can help you build a strong foundation for yours.

1. Pursue Academic Excellence

Your degree is the primary reason you are in school. Make learning your priority and strive to understand concepts rather than memorizing facts simply to pass examinations. Good grades may not guarantee success, but they open many doors and demonstrate discipline, commitment, and resilience.

Academic excellence is the foundation upon which you build everything else.

2. Identify Your Area of Interest

You cannot prepare for every opportunity, but you can prepare well for the opportunities that align with your interests. Take time to discover what excites you. Read widely, attend seminars, explore different fields, volunteer in projects, and speak with lecturers and professionals. The earlier you identify your direction, the easier it becomes to make intentional decisions about your growth.

3. Build Skills That Matter

A degree tells people what you studied. Skills show them what you can do.

Develop both technical and transferable skills that are relevant to your field. Learn to communicate effectively, solve problems, work in teams, analyse data, use digital tools, and adapt to new challenges. Every new skill increases your value.

4. Gain Experience in Your Field

Knowledge becomes more meaningful when you apply it.

Look for internships, volunteer opportunities, research projects, competitions, student organizations, and community initiatives that allow you to put your learning into practice. Experience builds confidence and helps you discover what you enjoy.

5. Take Initiative

Do not wait for opportunities to find you.

Apply for scholarships, internships, conferences, workshops, exchange programs, leadership positions, and competitions. If an opportunity does not exist, create one. Start a project, organize a study group, begin a research initiative, or identify a problem in your community and become part of the solution.

Students who stand out are often those who take the first step.

6. Learn Beyond the Classroom

Your lectures are only the beginning.

Read books, complete online courses, attend webinars, follow experts in your field, and stay informed about new developments. The most outstanding students never stop learning.

7. Find Mentors and Build Meaningful Relationships

No one succeeds alone.

Seek mentors who challenge you, guide you, and encourage your growth. Build genuine relationships with lecturers, researchers, professionals, and classmates. The right network can provide wisdom, encouragement, collaboration, and opportunities.

Remember, your network can influence your net worth. Surround yourself with people who inspire you to grow academically, professionally, and personally.

8. Build a Reputation for Excellence

People remember students they can trust.

Be reliable. Keep your promises. Meet deadlines. Be humble enough to learn, courageous enough to ask questions, and willing to help others. Over time, your reputation will become one of your greatest assets.

Standing out is not the result of one extraordinary achievement. It is the outcome of consistently making ordinary choices with extraordinary commitment.

Reflection

Take a moment to evaluate yourself.

  • Which of these principles am I already practising consistently?
  • Which area needs the most improvement?
  • If I continue with my current habits, where will I be one year from now?

Remember, growth does not happen by accident. It happens through intentional action.

My Takeaway

Every step I take today brings me closer to becoming the student and professional I aspire to be.

International Internship Opportunities

Many students do not realize that they can gain international experience while they are still in school. Internship opportunities are available at universities, research institutes, companies, NGOs, international organizations, and other institutions around the world.

Some international internships are fully funded and may provide a stipend, accommodation, flight support, health insurance, meals, or assistance with the visa application. Others may be partially funded, self-funded, paid, or unpaid. Always read the official programme information carefully to understand what is covered.

Yes, Fully Funded Internships Exist!

Your financial background should not automatically stop you from searching and applying. Some programmes are specifically designed to support talented students who may not be able to finance an international experience independently.

Opportunities may be awarded based on academic performance, skills, achievements, potential, financial need, motivation, or interest in a particular field. Some programmes also support students from low- and middle-income countries, the Global South, or communities affected by conflict and displacement.

Types of International Internships

Research Internships

Research internships allow students to work on scientific or academic projects at universities, laboratories, and research institutes. Interns may participate in experiments, data analysis, literature reviews, scientific writing, fieldwork, or research presentations under the guidance of a supervisor.

These opportunities are available to undergraduate, master’s, doctoral, and sometimes recently graduated students.

Summer Research Programmes

Summer research programmes are short, structured internships usually completed during university holidays. Many last between six and twelve weeks and include a research project, seminars, workshops, networking activities, and a final presentation.

Some summer programmes are fully funded and may cover travel, accommodation, and living expenses.

Industry Internships

Industry internships provide practical experience in companies and professional organizations. They allow students to apply what they have learned in school to real workplace projects and challenges.

These internships are available in biotechnology, pharmaceuticals, engineering, technology, finance, marketing, project management, operations, and many other sectors.

NGO and Development Internships

NGOs, foundations, humanitarian organizations, and development agencies offer internships related to public health, education, human rights, sustainability, policy, community development, humanitarian support, and social impact.

Some opportunities are designed specifically for students from low- and middle-income countries or the Global South.

Global Health Internships

Global health internships expose students to public health, disease prevention, health policy, epidemiology, community health, health education, and international health programmes.

They may be offered by universities, hospitals, research institutes, NGOs, government agencies, and international organizations.

International Organization Internships

International organizations offer internships in areas such as health, education, science, development, policy, economics, communications, administration, and humanitarian work.

Some positions are paid or provide an allowance, while others may be unpaid. Always check the funding conditions before applying.

Virtual and Remote Internships

Virtual internships allow students to gain international experience without travelling. Interns may work with teams, companies, laboratories, or organizations in another country while completing their responsibilities online.

They can be a flexible and accessible option for students who cannot travel or relocate.

Internship and Thesis Opportunities

Some universities and research institutes allow students to complete an internship, research project, or thesis abroad. These opportunities can help students gain specialized experience while completing part of their degree requirements.

Students should first confirm whether their home university will recognize the project or thesis.
